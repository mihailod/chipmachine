## App Store Mandatory Rejection Factors
### Key Blocker 1: Missing Hardened Runtime (`runtime` flag)

Your `codesign` output shows:

CodeDirectory v=20400 size=38353 flags=0x2(adhoc)

For Mac App Store submissions, Apple strictly requires the **Hardened Runtime** flag (`0x10000(runtime)` or `flags=0x10000(runtime)`).
Without `--options runtime` passed during codesign, `altool` / App Store Connect will instantly reject the package during ingestion.

---

## App Store Mandatory Rejection Factors
### Key Blocker 2: Orphaned Dynamic Libraries (`libcrypto.3.dylib`, `libssl.3.dylib`, `libpng16.16.dylib`)

* **OpenSSL (`libcrypto`/`libssl`):** Your main binary links to `/usr/lib/libcurl.4.dylib`, but `libcrypto.3.dylib` and `libssl.3.dylib` are physically present in `Contents/Frameworks/`. If your executable doesn't explicitly link them (or if `libcurl` isn't compiled to link them via `@rpath`), Apple's automated scanner will flag them. If they are unused, **delete them**. If they *are* used, you MUST add `<key>ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption</key>` to your `Info.plist`.
* **PNG (`libpng16.16.dylib`):** `libpng16` is sitting in `Frameworks/`, but it does not appear in `otool -L` for `chipmachine`. Unlinked dylibs bundled inside an app package trigger static analysis warnings during ingest.

---

## App Store Mandatory Rejection Factors
### Key Blocker 3: Missing Export Compliance Key in `Info.plist`

Even if you remove OpenSSL, your app links `/usr/lib/libcurl.4.dylib`. You need to explicitly set encryption compliance in `Info.plist` to prevent TestFlight / Store ingestion holds:

<key>ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption</key>

## App Store Mandatory Rejection Factors
### Key Blocker 4: Document Type Extension Fallback (`LSItemContentTypes`)

In your `Info.plist`, Document Type #1 uses `CFBundleTypeExtensions` instead of conforming to Uniform Type Identifiers (`LSItemContentTypes`):

"CFBundleTypeExtensions" => [ "208", "2dx", ... ]

Since macOS Catalina (and strictly enforced in macOS 11+ App Store guidelines), file association declarations relying solely on `CFBundleTypeExtensions` without corresponding `UTImportedTypeDeclarations` or `UTExportedTypeDeclarations` are deprecated and trigger submission warnings or rejection for custom/legacy file extensions.

## 1. INSTANT REJECTION: Dynamic Library Linking & Cross-Dependencies

Look closely at your `otool -L` output for internal Frameworks:

@loader_path/libssl.3.dylib
@loader_path/libcrypto.3.dylib
...
@loader_path/libpng16.16.dylib

### What is broken:

1. **`libssl.3.dylib` and `libcrypto.3.dylib`:** They are actively linked by other dynamic libraries in your bundle (e.g., `libcurl` or your FFmpeg libraries) via `@loader_path`. Because OpenSSL is physically linked and loaded, **Apple’s static analyzer will flag your submission for missing export compliance paperwork**.
2. **`libpng16.16.dylib`:** It is linked via `@loader_path` inside `libcrypto.3.dylib`.

### The Fix:

You MUST add the explicit encryption declaration to your `Info.plist` to prevent your app from being placed on hold upon submission:

<key>ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption</key>
<false/>

## 2. INSTANT REJECTION: Document Types vs. UTI Mismatch

Your exported UTI (`org.mihailod.chipmachine.chiptune`) in `UTExportedTypeDeclarations` explicitly declares hundreds of raw extensions (`208`, `2dx`, `mod`, `xm`, `sid`, `s3m`, etc.).

**However, look back at your `Info.plist` document types:**

* Document Type #1 maps `org.mihailod.chipmachine.chiptune`.
* Document Type #2 maps raw extensions (`CFBundleTypeExtensions`) directly without UTI references.

### What is broken:

Mac App Store validation enforces **strict UTI mapping**. Standard raw `CFBundleTypeExtensions` in `CFBundleDocumentTypes` without a mapped `LSItemContentTypes` are flagged as deprecated and rejected during App Store Connect ingestion.

### The Fix:

Delete Document Type #2 (`CFBundleTypeExtensions`) completely from your `Info.plist`. Since all extensions are already mapped inside your `UTExportedTypeDeclarations` under `org.mihailod.chipmachine.chiptune`, Document Type #1 covers every single one of those tracker extensions legally.
